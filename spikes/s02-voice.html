Model loading

Empirically confirmed 2026-08-08 (headless Chrome + real model files, before this page existed): the KaldiRecognizer(sampleRate, grammar) grammar parameter genuinely restricts output on vosk-model-small-it-0.22 — an out-of-vocabulary word ("undici") and an unrelated sentence ("buongiorno come stai") were both forced into the closed vocabulary (→ "dieci", → "sei") instead of being transcribed freely. Without a grammar, the same inputs were transcribed correctly/freely. Caveat: the [unk] reject token never fired in that test — out-of-vocabulary speech gets forced to the nearest in-vocabulary word rather than reliably rejected. Treat [unk] as unreliable for the "reject class" requirement; a confidence/duration heuristic will likely be needed on top. Catalan update (2026-08-08, real Montse/ca_ES TTS via vosk-model-small-ca-0.4): grammar restriction is confirmed here too (in-vocab words transcribed correctly with or without a grammar; an unrelated sentence "bon dia com estàs" was forced into the closed vocabulary). Notably different from Italian: [unk] did fire correctly for that out-of-vocabulary sentence on the Catalan model — so the reject-class reliability may be model-specific, not a general vosk-browser property; don't assume Italian's [unk] behavior generalizes to other languages.
CORS gotcha (also confirmed empirically): alphacephei.com (the official Vosk model host) sends no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header, so this page cannot fetch the Italian or Catalan models directly from there — the browser fetch fails with a generic "Failed to fetch". The default Italian and Catalan model URLs below point at same-origin copies in spikes/models/ instead (fetched once via spikes/models/fetch-it-model.sh / fetch-ca-model.sh; the .zip works as-is, no .tar.gz conversion needed — the extractor auto-detects format). The English model defaults to a mirror that does send permissive CORS headers, so it loads straight from its CDN with no self-hosting.

    Streaming probe (R9 — critical)

    Simulates the product's incremental capture window: for each utterance, the first 80% of its samples are fed to the recognizer incrementally in 4096-sample chunks (as they'd arrive from a live mic ring buffer) before a simulated "window close" timestamp. At that timestamp we feed the remaining 20% + call retrieveFinalResult(), and time from the timestamp to the resulting result event. This isolates post-close compute (parsing/finalization), which is what the ≤70 ms product budget is actually spent on — pre-close feeding has its own (separate) budget and doesn't count here, per R9's intent.

    Human-ambiguity check

    Kill-only condition: if a human can't reliably tell the recorded words apart, a recognizer never will — that indicts the corpus, not vosk. Plays a random stored utterance; you guess the word blind. Target: ≥95% human accuracy over a reasonable sample (n≥20 recommended).
