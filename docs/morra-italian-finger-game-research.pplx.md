# Morra — The Italian Finger Game: Research Documentation

## Purpose and scope

This document consolidates deep, source-cited research on morra — the Mediterranean hand game in which two players simultaneously throw out fingers while calling a number, with the correct guesser of the total scoring a point — as groundwork for a future mobile digitization of the game. It covers history and etymology, geographic distribution and regional variants, detailed game mechanics and rulesets, and the digital/technical landscape (existing apps, synchronization architecture, AI opponent design, and game theory). Every factual claim is linked to the source it was drawn from; where sources conflict or a claim could not be independently confirmed, this is flagged explicitly rather than presented as settled fact. This discipline matters for an app: several widely repeated "facts" about morra (a specific ban by a Roman official, a named Egyptian tomb, a Chinese transmission via Marco Polo, a national Italian federation called "Federmorra") turned out to be unverifiable or simply wrong, and the design implications throughout this report depend on knowing which rules are genuinely universal versus regional.

## Disambiguation: morra vs. "morra cinese"

Before anything else, one naming collision must be resolved for app scoping and App Store discoverability. "Morra" (also *mora*, *murra*, *mourre*, *mourra*, ancient *micatio*) is the numeral finger-guessing game covered in this entire report: players simultaneously throw a hand showing 0–5 fingers and shout a guess at the sum of both hands ([Wikipedia, Morra (game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game))). "Morra cinese" is the Italian name for rock-paper-scissors — an unrelated game with three hand shapes, no number calling, and no summation — named only for its superficial resemblance to morra and its presumed Chinese origin ([Wikipedia IT, Morra cinese](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_cinese)). Searching app stores for "Morra" mostly surfaces rock-paper-scissors apps for this reason ([mabi77 Morra, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Mabi77.com.unity.template.mobile2D&hl=en_US); [Morra Cinese, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=it.xabaras.android.morracinese.beta&hl=en_US)) — a naming and ASO risk worth planning around from day one.

## Part I: History, Origins and Etymology

### Ancient origins — contested and probably overstated

Reference sources across Italian, Spanish, and Catalan encyclopedias repeat that the earliest evidence of the game comes from ancient Egypt, typically citing a tomb of a "high court dignitary of the 25th Dynasty" ([Wikipedia IT, Morra](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/); [Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [Wikipedia CA](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra)). English Wikipedia gives more specific but contradictory references — the Theban tombs, Beni Hassan tomb 9, and the 26th-dynasty tomb of Aba ([Wikipedia EN, Morra (game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game))). Other sources date the same claimed Egyptian evidence to 700 BC ([Murramundo](https://www.murramundo.com/?lang=it)), 600 BC ([IPCITE](https://ipcite.cat/inventari/4/57)), or "around the 2nd millennium BC" ([Decreto 94/2023, Gobierno de Aragón](https://www.boa.aragon.es/cgi-bin/EBOA/BRSCGI?CMD=VEROBJ&MLKOB=1283264130606)). No fetched source cites a primary Egyptological publication, an excavation report, or a museum object for any of these claims — they should be treated as inherited tradition, not established fact, in any app copy. The commonly cited "Greek vase painting of Helen and Paris playing morra" suffers the same problem: no museum, inventory number, or attribution for any such vase was found in any source ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [Decreto 94/2023](https://www.boa.aragon.es/cgi-bin/EBOA/BRSCGI?CMD=VEROBJ&MLKOB=1283264130606)).

Solid ground begins in ancient Rome. The game was called ***micatio***, and playing it was ***micare digitis*** — literally "to flash with the fingers" — with the modern name *morra* understood as a corruption of the verb *micare* ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game)); [Focus.it](https://www.focus.it/cultura/storia/quando-e-nato-il-gioco-della-morra-perche-e-vietato)). Suetonius (*Augustus* 13.2) records Augustus telling a condemned father and son to "cast lots or play mora" to decide who should be spared ([LacusCurtius, Suetonius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Augustus*.html)), and Cicero uses *micare* as a synonym for a fair lottery in *De Officiis* III.90 ([De officiis, Latin text](http://individual.utoronto.ca/pking/resources/cicero/De_officiis.txt)).

**The Cicero proverb — verified, but usually misquoted.** The famous line invoked as proof of the game's antiquity is genuine and appears at ***De Officiis*, Book III, §77***, in a passage where Cicero explains that people praise someone's good faith by saying he is a man "with whom you could play *micare* in the dark" — a mark of total trust. The transmitted Latin is *"dignum esse dicunt, quicum in tenebris mices"* ([De officiis, full Latin text, §3.77](http://individual.utoronto.ca/pking/resources/cicero/De_officiis.txt)). The form quoted everywhere in popular and even federation literature — *"dignus est quicum in tenebris mices"* — is a paraphrase, not Cicero's actual wording ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game)); [FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)). The same proverb reappears in Petronius, *Satyricon* XLIV, at Trimalchio's banquet ([Petronius, Latin text](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/petronius1.html)). A related, frequently repeated claim — that *micatio* was banned in the Roman forum by "Apronius, prefect of the city" — traces only to English Wikipedia and its mirrors; it is absent from Smith's authoritative *Dictionary* article on Roman gaming law, which lists every known Roman gaming statute (*lex Titia*, *lex Publicia*, *lex Cornelia*, the Senate decree, Justinian's ban) with no mention of morra or an Apronius ([LacusCurtius, "Alea"](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/secondary/SMIGRA*/Alea.html)). Treat it as unverified.

### Etymology: genuinely uncertain

Treccani, the standard Italian dictionary, states the etymology of *morra* as ***etimo incerto*** — uncertain ([Treccani, "morra"](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/morra/)). Competing hypotheses include derivation from the Roman *micatio*, from Celtic *meur* ("finger"), or introduction by the Moors/Arabs ([Wiktionary, "morra"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/morra); [Virgilio Sapere](https://sapere.virgilio.it/parole/vocabolario/morra)). A detailed 2024 philological review in the *Gazzetta di Parma* surveys and largely rejects the classical proposals, offering instead a new hypothesis that *giocare alla mòrra* is a popular deformation of an older *giocare all'amore*, attested in several Emilian dialects, with earliest Italian documentary attestations of *ludus morre* at Sant'Anatolia (1324) and Assisi (1469) ([Gazzetta di Parma, 1 June 2024](https://www.gazzettadiparma.it/home/2024/06/01/news/la-morra-giocare-alla-morra-o-giocare-all-amore-un-origine-controversa-789891/)). Importantly for app naming/localization, Spanish and Catalan *morra* meaning "top of the head" is a **separate, unrelated lexical entry** — the game sense of *morra* in both languages is borrowed directly from Italian, not derived from the anatomical sense, per the Real Academia Española and Wiktionary ([DLE/RAE](https://dle.rae.es/morra); [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/morra)). Regional dialect names collected across the research include *murra* (Sardinia, Calabria, Sicily), *mora* (Piedmont, Trentino), *mourra/mourre* (Aosta Valley, Provence, Nice), *šijavica* (Croatia/Istria), and Arabic *mukharaja* ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game))).

### Historical spread and the China question

Italian, Catalan, and Aragonese heritage sources credit Roman legions with spreading the game across Europe, the Near East, and North Africa as they colonized ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game)); [Decreto 94/2023](https://www.boa.aragon.es/cgi-bin/EBOA/BRSCGI?CMD=VEROBJ&MLKOB=1283264130606)). The user's starting source, the Morràpita association's history page, includes a stage titled "To China with Marco Polo," claiming Venetian Silk Road trade carried morra to China, where it became known as "Chai Moi" ([Morràpita, "Història"](https://www.morrapita.cat/historia)). This transmission claim is **not supported by the Chinese record**: the Chinese finger-guessing tradition (猜拳 *cāi quán*, 划拳 *huá quán*) is independently and thoroughly documented as an indigenous development from Ming-dynasty drinking-game literature, with no reference to any Western game in the Chinese-language sources fetched ([Wikipedia ZH, 猜拳](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hk/%E7%8C%9C%E6%8B%B3); [Taiwan Panorama](https://www.taiwan-panorama.com/en/Articles/Details?Guid=fa7bfabe-817f-4790-b9d6-daa4624a245e&CatId=11&postname=Guess-Fingers:%20A%20Bout%20for%20Barflies)). The safest statement for app copy is that structurally similar finger-guessing games arose independently in the Mediterranean and in China, with no evidenced historical link between them, notwithstanding the claim made by some heritage associations.

Diaspora communities carried the game to the Americas: the Catalan source itself notes emigrants took morra to the USA and Argentina ([Morràpita, "Història"](https://www.morrapita.cat/historia)), and this is independently confirmed by a documented Pennsylvania coal-town tradition (Heilwood, with a 1951 photograph) ([Heilwood history](https://www.heilwood.com/people/fun-games/morra/)) and an active Argentine championship in Colonia Caroya, Córdoba ([La Voz](https://www.lavoz.com.ar/supletemas/la-mora-un-juego-de-manos/)). Brazil represents a significant, under-documented diaspora case: "jogo da mora" survives in the Talian/Vêneto language of Rio Grande do Sul, brought by Venetian emigrants, with documented sub-variants *mora sonada*, *mora ciamada*, *mora muta*, and others ([Wikipedia PT, Jogo da mora](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogo_da_mora)).

### The starting source in context: morrapita.cat/historia

The Morràpita association's history timeline — the page the research began from — presents eleven narrative stages: Egyptian beginnings (25th Dynasty), Ancient Greece, the Roman Empire (with the Cicero quote), the Almogavars, transmission to China with Marco Polo, corsairs playing while lying in wait at sea, morra as a currency of exchange between merchants, a Romanticist-era literary reference (a story attributed to the poet Àngel Guimerà), a 1900s photograph of children playing in Nice, transmission to the Americas with Italian emigrants, and a final "Today" section ([Morràpita, "Història"](https://www.morrapita.cat/historia)). This page is valuable as a snapshot of the association's own promotional narrative and should be represented as such in an app's "history" section, but it supplies no citations of its own, and several of its strongest claims (the Egyptian dynasty, the China transmission, the Guimerà story's title) could not be independently verified by this research and in the China case are actively contradicted by the Chinese primary record. A companion interview with the same association gives a different, softer origin hypothesis ("could be in Turkey," with only "indications of a very similar game" in China and Thailand) ([Morràpita interview](https://www.morrapita.cat/post/en-%C3%A8poca-de-corsaris-la-morra-s-usava-com-a-moneda-de-canvi)), underscoring that even the association's own materials do not present a single settled origin story.

### Art, literature, and bans

Morra appears repeatedly in European visual art, most notably Karel Dujardin's mid-17th-century *Les Joueurs de "morra"* at the Louvre (inv. RF 2002 1) ([Louvre collections](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010067294)) and an early-1620s Caravaggesque canvas at the Pinacoteca Nazionale di Siena ([Pinacoteca di Siena](https://www.pinacotecanazionalesiena.it/portfolio/dono-giulio-grisaldi-del-taia-di-siena/)). Literary attestations run from Manzoni's *I promessi sposi* to Rabelais's *Gargantua* and *Pantagruel*, and the game appears in Jean Vigo's 1930 film *À propos de Nice* and Bertolucci's *1900* ([Wikipedia FR, Mourre](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourre)).

Legally, morra has a genuinely fraught history that any app framing should get right. It was banned in public places in the Republic of Venice from the 15th century (though tolerated in taverns and private clubs) ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra)), and it was formally prohibited across Italy in **1929/1931** under the Fascist regime as a game of chance associated with quarrels and brawls ([FriuliOggi](https://www.friulioggi.it/friuli-venezia-giulia/morra-torna-legale-friuli-venezia-giulia-15-giugno-2024/); [Associazione Giochi Antichi](https://www.associazionegiochiantichi.it/comunita-di-gioco/morra/)). **This ban remains technically in force today** across most of Italy, under Article 110 of the TULPS (Testo Unico delle Leggi di Pubblica Sicurezza) — morra is still listed in the national table of prohibited games ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)). It has been struck from that list only in the autonomous province of Trento (2001) and, on a one-year experimental basis with no money betting, in Friuli Venezia Giulia from 7 June 2024 ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [FriuliOggi](https://www.friulioggi.it/friuli-venezia-giulia/morra-torna-legale-friuli-venezia-giulia-15-giugno-2024/)). This explains why so much of today's organized morra activity happens through cultural associations and heritage festivals rather than casual bar play, and it is a genuinely interesting piece of framing for an app ("a game so intense it's still technically illegal to play for money in most Italian bars").

Two important corrections belong permanently in any app's "about" content. First, **"Federmorra" does not exist** — no such body appears in any source; the real Italian governing body is **FIGeST**, the Federazione Italiana Giochi e Sport Tradizionali ([FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)). Second, **morra itself is not UNESCO-inscribed** — the 2022 UNESCO inscription covers the "Tocatì" programme for safeguarding traditional games generally (jointly submitted by Italy, Belgium, Croatia, Cyprus, and France), not morra specifically; Italian Wikipedia's claim of a direct 2023 UNESCO inscription of morra is uncorroborated ([UNESCO ICH](https://ich.unesco.org/en/BSP/tocati-a-shared-programme-for-the-safeguarding-of-traditional-games-and-sports-01709); [Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra)).

## Part II: Geographic Distribution and Regional Variants

Morra's documented range spans the whole of Italy, the western Alps, and the western Mediterranean from southern Spain through southern France and Corsica to Croatia, with additional attestation in Greece, Portugal, Cyprus, Malta, Istria, Dalmatia, and Herzegovina ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game))). The single most useful map of where the game is *actually organized* today, rather than merely historically attested, is the roster of roughly twenty delegations that compete in the Murramundo world championship circuit (see below).

### Italy

Morra is a CONI-recognized traditional sport through **FIGeST**, whose match format is *partita – rivincita – bella* (game – rematch – decider, best of three) ([FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)). The national championship rotates host regions annually; its 2026 edition (20th, at Acquaviva di Cagli, Marche) drew competitors from eight regions — Marche, Abruzzo, Lazio, Basilicata, Trentino-Alto Adige, Lombardia, Emilia-Romagna, and Campania — with categories for singles, pairs, and trios ([ViverUrbino](https://www.vivereurbino.it/2026/07/28/ad-acquaviva-di-cagli-il-20-campionato-italiano-assoluto-di-morra-domina-il-lazio/133640/)). Regional strongholds documented in depth include:

- **Sardinia ("sa murra")**, widely described as the homeland of morra in Italy, with its own referee title (*su hontadòre*), its own number-word set culminating in the taboo word *mùrra* instead of "ten," and the Campionau sardu de sa murra (25+ editions) run by the Sòciu po su giocu de sa murra association since 1998 ([Mamoiada.org PDF](https://www.mamoiada.org/_pdf/_lagente/murra.pdf); [L'Unione Sarda](https://www.unionesarda.it/3-minuti-con/il-giubileo-del-campionato-sardo-di-morra-da-urzulei-alla-ribalta-internazionale-cr5t27ap)).
- **Lazio/Rome ("morra romana")**, the classic *osteria* game, with rich documented terminology (*la riavuta* for a rematch, *la bella* for the decider, *la cinquina* for reaching five points) and currently the strongest competitive Italian region, through the ASD ALAM club ([Portale delle Osterie](http://www.portaledelleosterie.it/osterie_dettaglio.php?Main=20&Sub=131&Ter=133); [ViverUrbino](https://www.vivereurbino.it/2026/07/28/ad-acquaviva-di-cagli-il-20-campionato-italiano-assoluto-di-morra-domina-il-lazio/133640/)).
- **Friuli Venezia Giulia**, home of the Circolo Friulano della Morra (Udine, founded 1980s) and the region where the public-premises ban was lifted in 2024 ([FriuliOggi](https://www.friulioggi.it/friuli-venezia-giulia/morra-torna-legale-friuli-venezia-giulia-15-giugno-2024/)).
- **Marche**, home of the AMAM association, which publishes the most useful catalogue of named Italian and foreign variants (see Part III).
- **Trentino** and **Valle d'Aosta**, each with FIGeST-affiliated regional bodies, the latter with counting in Francoprovençal (*eun, do, trë, càtro, tchisse...*) shared with neighboring Savoie and Valais ([fr.wikipedia, Mourre](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourre)).
- **Piedmont**, whose morra scene is chiefly a Sardinian-diaspora phenomenon in Turin, including a schools numeracy project using the game ([Tottus in Pari](https://www.tottusinpari.it/2018/10/14/il-gioco-della-morra-per-sviluppare-le-abilita-numeriche-progetto-a-torino-dallassociazione-gramsci-con-le-scuole-piemontesi/)).

Several regions (Liguria, Tuscany, Sicily, Emilia-Romagna) are attested only as Murramundo delegations or in historical mentions, with no dedicated federation or standing championship found — the research explicitly flags these as unconfirmed rather than invented.

### France

*Mourre* has been on the French national Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage since 2012 ([fr.wikipedia, Mourre](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourre)). France has exactly **three official delegations**: Savoie, Pays Niçois, and Corsica ([Savoie News](https://www.savoie-news.fr/reportages/culture-loisirs/article/la-mourra-l-rsquo-un-des-plus-vieux-jeux-du-monde)). The Nice-area association "Mourra dei Quatre Cantouns" (founded 2003 at Ilonse by Pascal Colletta, who also coined the name "Mourramoundo") is where the international championship's name originates ([fr.wikipedia, Pascal Colletta](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Colletta)). Corsica has an active association (Corsica Morra, since 2011) and a museum-backed annual competition at Corti ([Musée de la Corse](https://www.museudiacorsica.corsica/fr/nos-expositions/programmation-culturelle/)). Notably, the French "règle du cinq" variant permits the closed fist to count as zero, giving sums 0–10 rather than the Italian standard 2–10 — a genuine rules divergence, not just a naming one ([de.wikipedia, Morra (Spiel)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(Spiel))).

### The international circuit: Murramundo

This is the de facto world championship and the best single indicator of where organized morra genuinely exists today. Created in 2003 by the Sardinian association as *s'Atòbiu de sos murradores de su Mediterràneu* and renamed **Murramundo** in 2014, it draws roughly twenty delegations: Aragón, Basilicata, Calabria, Catalonia, Corsica, Nice, Croatia/Istria, Friuli, Lazio, Liguria, Marche, Valencia, Piedmont, Sardinia, Savoy, Sicily, Slovenia/Istria, Tuscany, Trentino, Tunisia, Valle d'Aosta, and Veneto ([Murramundo](https://www.murramundo.com/?lang=it)). Recent hosts include Corsica (2019), Savoie (2022), Sardinia (2023, 19th edition, ~200 players), Catalonia/La Ràpita (2024, 20th edition), and Slovenia (2025) ([Murramundo](https://www.murramundo.com/?lang=it); [El Temps](https://www.eltemps.cat/article/61135/morra-el-joc-de-la-camorra-que-aspira-a-patrimoni-de-la-humanitat)). The movement is actively pursuing UNESCO Representative List status for morra itself (distinct from the existing Tocatì programme inscription) ([El Temps](https://www.eltemps.cat/article/61135/morra-el-joc-de-la-camorra-que-aspira-a-patrimoni-de-la-humanitat)).

### Catalonia and Spain

**Morràpita**, based at Sant Carles de la Ràpita, is the association behind the user's starting source. Founded in 2007/formalized 2009, it is a founding member of the Catalan Federació de Joc Esport i Deport Tradicional and runs roughly a dozen annual championships, playing to 10 points ([Morràpita, "Qui som"](https://www.morrapita.cat/qui-som); [Xarxanet](https://xarxanet.org/noticies/cultural/rafa-balague-el-joc-tradicional-et-connecta-amb-la-teva-herencia-la-terra-i-la-cultura)). Independent Catalan press confirms morra is played, within Catalonia, only at Sant Carles de la Ràpita and the Ebre delta ([ens.cat](https://www.ens.cat/noticia/la-morra-un-joc-millenari-que-reviu-a-la-rpita-326)). Outside Catalonia, **Teruel (Aragón)** is Spain's other major stronghold: the game was declared a Bien de Interés Cultural Inmaterial of Aragón in June 2023, and its provincial championship (30th edition in 2025) publishes a full winners table back to 1992 ([lamorra.es](https://www.lamorra.es/); [lamorra.es, Campeonatos](https://lamorra.es/campeonatos)).

### Other Mediterranean countries and the diaspora

Croatia's Istrian/Dalmatian variant, **šijavica**, is well documented: numbers are called in corrupted Italian, the game is exclusively male by tradition (women historically barred even from watching), and it is played at a table in a tavern (*konoba*) ([hr.wikipedia, Šijavica](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0ijavica)). Switzerland's Romansh Engadin has an active tradition (club at Zernez, five rounds of eleven points), reportedly arriving with Italian railway workers ([Allegra](https://www.allegra.online/nr04-sommer-23/regiunal-special/1133-murra-das-spiel-um-eine-handvoll-finger)). In the diaspora, the **Youngstown Morra League** (Ohio, ~35 years old, 24 teams/4 divisions) and the **Italian Fingers Championship** (official since 2005, team format, legs to 12, no zero permitted) are the best-documented US scenes ([La Gazzetta Italiana](https://www.lagazzettaitaliana.com/local-news/8520-morra-alive-and-well-in-the-mahoning-valley); [italianfingers.com](https://www.italianfingers.com/)). Argentina's Colonia Caroya championship draws up to 300 pairs and teaches the game in four local schools ([La Voz](https://www.lavoz.com.ar/supletemas/la-mora-un-juego-de-manos/)). No evidence was found for organized play in Uruguay, Australia, or the Canary Islands — these should not appear in app copy as playing regions.

### Explicit gaps and corrections carried forward from the geographic research

Beyond "Federmorra" and the UNESCO misattribution already noted, the geographic research specifically checked and could not confirm a claimed morra championship at San Vito di Cadore (Belluno) — the only confirmed Belluno-area connection is a variant called "Morrina," recorded at Mel ([giocodellamorra.it, Varianti](https://www.giocodellamorra.it/varianti.htm)). This is a useful cautionary example: association and Wikipedia claims about specific places and events should be spot-checked against primary sources before being embedded in app content, since several such claims did not survive verification.

## Part III: Game Mechanics and Rules

### The core mechanic, step by step

1. Two players face each other, either seated at a table (northern Italy, Valle d'Aosta, France) or standing (Rome, southern Italy, Friuli) ([Regolamento Gioco della Morra in Valle d'Aosta, 2016](http://www.fentvda.it/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Regolamento-Gioco-Morra-in-VdA-2016.pdf); [Regolamento Generale del Gioco della Morra](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm)).
2. Each player holds one hand in a raised, closed fist ([Treccani, Morra](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/morra_(Enciclopedia-Italiana)/)).
3. On a shared beat, both players simultaneously extend some number of fingers and, in the same instant, shout a guess at the total ([Wikipedia IT, Morra](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra)).
4. If exactly one player's called number equals the sum of both players' shown fingers, that player scores a point ([FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)).
5. If both call correctly, or neither does, no point is awarded and play continues immediately into the next throw, at roughly one call per second, with no pause between throws ([Regolamento Generale, rules 11 and 14](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm)).
6. Play continues until a pre-agreed target score is reached.

### Finger range and call range — the single most important configurable rule

There is genuine, codified regional disagreement here, and an app must not hard-code one answer.

- **Standard Italian competition rule**: each hand shows 1–5 fingers (a **closed fist counts as 1, not 0**), so calls run 2–10; ten may be called as *dieci*, *morra*, or *tutta* ([Regolamento Generale, rules 2–3](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm); [Marche rulebook PDF](https://www.giocodellamorra.it/regolamento.pdf)).
- **French "règle du cinq"**: the fist can represent zero, so hands run 0–5 and calls run 0–10, giving 36 possible combinations versus 25 in the no-zero version ([Fes la Mourra — Règle du jeu](http://mourra.unblog.fr/regle-du-jeu/)).
- **Sardinian folk description**: sources conflict even within the same tradition — one says "zero does not exist" (1–5 only), another describes 0–5 explicitly ([Sardinia Point](https://sardiniapoint.it/un-isola-un-continente/la-murra-sarda-le-regole-del-gioco/); [Make Heritage Fun](https://www.makeheritagefun.com/make-murra-not-war/)).

**Design implication:** ship a "fist = 0 or 1" toggle as a core rules setting, not an afterthought.

### Team play: "holding the hand"

This is the single most consistent team mechanic across every regional source and must be implemented correctly rather than as a simple round-robin. Only two players throw at any moment, one per team. The player who wins a point immediately faces the next opponent on the other team and keeps playing as long as they keep winning; the moment they lose a point, their own teammate takes over ([FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/); [Fes la Mourra](http://mourra.unblog.fr/regle-du-jeu/); [Mamoiada PDF](https://www.mamoiada.org/_pdf/_lagente/murra.pdf)). The consequence — a single dominant player can in principle beat an entire opposing team alone — is treated as a real and celebrated feature of the game, not a bug ([Portale delle Osterie](http://www.portaledelleosterie.it/osterie_dettaglio.php?Main=20&Sub=131&Ter=133)).

### Scoring targets — must be configurable, not fixed

No universal target score exists. Documented targets include 10 (Catalonia), 11 per round (Swiss Engadin), 12 (IFC, USA), 15 (France, Trentino), 16 (Sardinia, general Italian rules, most common single figure), and 21 (Italian *bella*/decider, Friuli, Teruel's two-game format, Argentina's chalk-mark count) ([Xarxanet](https://xarxanet.org/noticies/cultural/rafa-balague-el-joc-tradicional-et-connecta-amb-la-teva-herencia-la-terra-i-la-cultura); [Allegra](https://www.allegra.online/nr04-sommer-23/regiunal-special/1133-murra-das-spiel-um-eine-handvoll-finger); [italianfingers.com](https://www.italianfingers.com/); [Mamoiada PDF](https://www.mamoiada.org/_pdf/_lagente/murra.pdf); [giocopopolare.it](https://www.giocopopolare.it/morra.html); [es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [La Voz](https://www.lavoz.com.ar/supletemas/la-mora-un-juego-de-manos/)). The standard Italian competitive **match structure** is *partita – rivincita – bella* — game, rematch, decider — won by whoever takes two of the three ([FIGeST](https://figest.it/morra/)). English Wikipedia's claim of "first to 3 points" as the "most popular version" is an outlier unsupported by any federation ruleset fetched and should not be treated as the default.

### Anti-cheating and officiating

Because the game moves at roughly one call per second, cheating (changing the shown fingers a fraction of a second after seeing the opponent's hand) is a real, acknowledged risk in the physical game, and every fetched ruleset addresses it directly: hands must be clearly visible to players and referees; changing the finger count after the hands are extended is explicitly forbidden and sanctionable; three warnings cost the offending player the point; referee decisions are absolutely final ([Regolamento Generale, rules 12–16](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm); [VdA 2016, rule 11](http://www.fentvda.it/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Regolamento-Gioco-Morra-in-VdA-2016.pdf)). Regional officiating titles include Italian *arbitro*, Sardinian *su hontadòre*/*su contadori*, and the Trentino *"la siora"* (an informal scorekeeper rather than a judge) ([Mamoiada PDF](https://www.mamoiada.org/_pdf/_lagente/murra.pdf); [Il T Quotidiano](https://www.iltquotidiano.it/articoli/lorenzo-kessler-vi-spiego-i-segreti-della-morra-gioco-a-lungo-proibito/)). This entire category of physical-world cheating and refereeing — the hardest problem for the traditional game — is, notably, one that a digital implementation can eliminate outright through server-side simultaneous reveal (see Part IV).

### The "chiamata" — calling conventions and dialect

Numbers are typically shouted in local dialect, often shortened to a single syllable for speed, and codified rulesets generally require Italian in national championships while permitting agreed local dialects elsewhere ([Regolamento Generale, rule 10](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm); [VdA 2016, rules 13–14](http://www.fentvda.it/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Regolamento-Gioco-Morra-in-VdA-2016.pdf)). Distinct documented number-word sets include Sardinian (*Unu, Dus, Tresi... Murra!* for ten, never *Deghe*), Occitan (*un, dus, tres... morre*), Francoprovençal (*eun, do, trë... tchisse* for five, *dji* for ten), and Roman clipped forms ("Quattr!", "Cinq!", "Tutta!") ([L'Unione Sarda](https://www.unionesarda.it/bella-sardegna/leggende-e-tradizioni/sa-murra-tutto-sul-gioco-sardo-dove-si-contano-le-dita-whsey8ry); [fr.wikipedia, Mourre](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourre); [Portale delle Osterie](http://www.portaledelleosterie.it/osterie_dettaglio.php?Main=20&Sub=131&Ter=133)). Ritualized taunts and banter are explicitly permitted under FIGeST rules as "folkloristic" behavior when both sides agree ([Regolamento Generale, rule 17](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm)) — a strong candidate for a localized "voice pack" or emote feature in an app.

### Setting, equipment, and social ritual

Equipment is minimal by design — just two hands and a strong voice ([Fes la Mourra](http://mourra.unblog.fr/regle-du-jeu/)). The historical setting is overwhelmingly the tavern (*osteria*), with the traditional stake a litre of wine, and the loser conventionally paying for the next round ([Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(Spiel))). Scorekeeping methods vary picturesquely by region — fingers of the free hand, a referee's two fists, chalk marks on the table, or a token/pledge system in which players exchange physical tokens that must be reclaimed point by point ([Regolamento Generale, rule 26](https://www.roccasalli.it/regolamento_morra.htm)) — all of which are strong visual/UX references for an app's scoreboard design.

## Part IV: Mobile and Digital Adaptation Landscape

### Existing Morra apps — a real but tiny category

Digital morra already exists, confirming there is precedent but also that the category is extremely under-served. Notable examples:

| App | Platform | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SaMurra** | iOS/Android | React Native; 5K+ Play downloads; online play with bot fallback; deliberately ad-free with "no economic return" by design choice ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.samurra&hl=en_US); [S&H Magazine](https://www.shmag.it/tecnologia/app/18_03_2018/sa-murra-la-morra-sarda-diventa-unapp-grazie-al-talento-di-due-giovani-sardi/)) |
| **Morra vs AI** | iOS/macOS/visionOS | SHA-256 commit-reveal protocol, WebSocket real-time play, 6-model Bayesian ensemble AI, session audit viewer ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/morra-vs-ai/id6758920205)) |
| **morra.io** | Web | Configurable target score and team size; two-row five-button input; call row filtered to legal values only ([morra.io](https://morra.io/how-to-play/)) |
| **handgames.app** | Web | Simple vs-computer implementation with explicit commit-before-reveal framing ([handgames.app](https://handgames.app/morra/en/)) |

SaMurra's product history is the closest real-world precedent and worth learning from directly: it took roughly five years from prototype to shipping, deliberately compressed the traditional 16-point game into 3-round matches for mobile session lengths, and grew entirely through social sharing with zero ad spend; its unshipped roadmap (Facebook-friend leaderboards, online tournaments, AI difficulty levels) maps closely onto features a new app should prioritize ([La Nuova Sardegna](https://www.lanuovasardegna.it/regione/2018/01/19/news/sa-murra-e-digitale-con-un-app-si-gioca-sullo-smartphone-1.16371234); [S&H Magazine](https://www.shmag.it/tecnologia/app/18_03_2018/sa-murra-la-morra-sarda-diventa-unapp-grazie-al-talento-di-due-giovani-sardi/)). No existing app was found to offer local pass-and-play — a genuine, real gap, though one that is structurally awkward since pass-and-play breaks true simultaneity and needs a hidden-entry screen per player as the physical analogue of a commit phase.

### Solving simultaneity: the core technical problem

The classic failure mode is well documented in a public postmortem: an early rock-paper-scissors web game forwarded a player's move to the opponent's client before the round closed, letting anyone with browser devtools open read the opponent's choice and win every round ([r/SideProject postmortem](https://www.reddit.com/r/SideProject/comments/1v99jij/built_a_multiplayer_rock_paper_scissors_game_the/)). The fix generalizes directly to morra: the server must lock each player's move, send the opponent only a contentless "opponent has moved" event, and compute the winner server-side, never trusting the client. A clean, race-free implementation pattern comes from a documented serverless reference architecture: store the whole game state in one record, have each player atomically write their move and increment a shared counter, and resolve the round the instant that counter reaches two ([serialized.net](https://serialized.net/2020/09/multiplayer/); [jbarratt/rpsls, GitHub](https://github.com/jbarratt/rpsls)).

For trust-sensitive contexts (playing against an AI whose fairness a player might doubt, or wagered play), a cryptographic **commit-reveal scheme** is the established layer on top of server authority: one side publishes a hash of their move plus a random salt, plays proceed, and the hash is verified against the revealed move afterward. This is not theoretical for this exact game — a shipping app (Morra vs AI) already advertises SHA-256 commit-reveal with a full audit trail as a marketing feature ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/morra-vs-ai/id6758920205); conceptual background at [Telefónica Tech](https://telefonicatech.com/en/blog/rock-paper-scissors-commit-now-reveal-later)). Two known implementation pitfalls to design around from the start: weak or reused salts allow an opponent to precompute and match a predictable commitment before reveal, so the salt must be a fresh, cryptographically random value bound to the specific game and turn ([Cyfrin CodeHawks finding 189](https://codehawks.cyfrin.io/c/2025-04-rock-paper-scissors/s/189)); and a "selective reveal" exploit lets a second revealer see the first player's move and simply abstain (forcing a timeout) whenever they would lose, which requires a strict reveal window with an automatic forfeit-on-non-reveal rule ([Cyfrin CodeHawks finding 290](https://codehawks.cyfrin.io/c/2025-04-rock-paper-scissors/s/290)). Notably, the interesting bit is that morra's official competitive rule — "one challenger cannot play after seeing what the other has shown" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morra_(game))) — is exactly the commit-reveal requirement, just written in 19th-century tournament language rather than cryptographic terms.

### Input methods

The proven default is the **two-row, five-button interface** already shipped by morra.io: one row to select fingers, one row to select the call, with the call row automatically filtered to only legal/sensible values (fingers shown + 1 through 5), since a call below your own fingers or more than five above them can never win — this simultaneously prevents a whole class of beginner error and functions as a built-in tutorial ([morra.io](https://morra.io/how-to-play/)). This matches empirical findings that "showing a hand number smaller than the voice number" is a classic beginner mistake in the physical game ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541701/)). Camera-based gesture recognition is technically possible but non-trivial: Google's MediaPipe Gesture Recognizer runs efficiently on-device (roughly 17–21 ms per frame on a Pixel 6) but its built-in canned gesture set covers only seven shapes and cannot distinguish 3 or 4 extended fingers, so a morra app pursuing camera input would need a custom-trained classifier or raw hand-landmark counting rather than the out-of-the-box model ([Google AI Edge, Gesture Recognizer](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/gesture_recognizer)). Voice input for the traditional "shout" is culturally authentic but unverified in any existing app and is best treated as an optional flavor layer rather than the primary mechanic, especially given that expert-level physical play runs at more than one round per second ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541701/); [handgames.app](https://handgames.app/morra/en/)).

### AI opponent design

A sensible difficulty ladder emerges from the sources. At the floor, a uniformly random legal-move generator is a valid, unbeatable-in-expectation baseline, since game theory guarantees no strategy beats the equilibrium mix against a perfectly random opponent ([Algorand Developer Portal, Reach Morra tutorial](https://developer.algorand.org/solutions/morra-game-using-reach/); [0Zeta/RockPaperScissors, GitHub](https://github.com/0Zeta/RockPaperScissors)). One level up, simple frequency/n-gram opponent modeling is empirically effective against humans: a Stanford project using only the previous 1–4 moves won roughly 55% of rock-paper-scissors games versus 33% for random play, with no benefit from longer history ([CS229 report](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2015/246_report.pdf)). At the top, meta-strategy ensembles that combine multiple base models and detect the opponent's own strategy shifts (the "Iocaine Powder" family) represent the established state of the art for this genre of game ([CS229 report](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2015/246_report.pdf); [0Zeta/RockPaperScissors](https://github.com/0Zeta/RockPaperScissors)).

A morra-specific empirical finding is directly actionable for AI tuning: a 2020 study of Sardinian *sa murra* found that expert players generate near-random finger sequences during real play (redundancy 3.31%) while beginners are heavily patterned (redundancy 15.53%) — even though both groups perform similarly on an isolated, non-competitive random-number task ([Delogu et al., *Frontiers in Psychology* 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541701/)). The design implication is that a beginner-facing AI can profitably exploit detected patterns, while an expert-facing AI should fall back toward the mathematically optimal mixed strategy. The same study found experts have poor conscious awareness of their own move-frequency bias — suggesting a "here's what you actually threw" post-match statistics screen would be a genuinely novel, evidence-backed feature rather than a generic add-on.

### Game-theory foundations

The two-finger version of morra is a textbook zero-sum game taught in university game-theory courses specifically because it has a clean, provable solution: with payoffs of +2 (both throw 1), +4 (both throw 2), and −3 (mismatch), the unique equilibrium has both players throwing 1 finger with probability 7/12 and 2 fingers with probability 5/12, for a game value of −1/12 ([Stanford CS221](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2022-extra/modules/games/simultaneous-games-6pp.pdf); [UMass CMPSCI 240](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~mcgregor/240S17/lec21.pdf)). The three-finger extension has an equally clean solution: (1/4, 1/2, 1/4) for both players, game value exactly zero ([UMass CMPSCI 240](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~mcgregor/240S17/lec21.pdf)). This makes two- and three-finger morra genuinely excellent onboarding/tutorial modes for an app — simple enough to solve completely, with a provably optimal strategy that can be taught explicitly, before graduating players to the full 0–5/1–5 game. A 2024 physics paper even demonstrates a "quantum morra" using photonic qubits where the classical game's built-in fairness (a symmetric mixed equilibrium) can be broken, giving one player a provable advantage — an interesting stretch-goal curiosity feature rather than a core mechanic ([Ulibarrena et al., *Phys. Rev. Research* 2024](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.023248)).

### UX, matchmaking, and monetization patterns

For niche multiplayer games with thin concurrent player pools — which a morra app will certainly be — a bot fallback when no human opponent is available is not optional: SaMurra's own download growth is reported to have followed directly from adding this feature ([La Nuova Sardegna](https://www.lanuovasardegna.it/regione/2018/01/19/news/sa-murra-e-digitale-con-un-app-si-gioca-sullo-smartphone-1.16371234)). A clean separation between matchmaking (finding an opponent) and the lobby (coordinating the actual connection), plus a persistent player identity independent of the transport connection so dropped sessions can reconnect mid-game, are both documented best practices from mainstream multiplayer infrastructure (Microsoft PlayFab's lobby/matchmaking model; the reveal-counter architecture referenced above) ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/playfab/multiplayer/lobby/lobby-and-matchmaking); [serialized.net](https://serialized.net/2020/09/multiplayer/)). On monetization, the observed pattern among existing morra apps splits between ad-supported free apps and deliberately ad-free passion projects, with no evidence yet of a successful paid or subscription morra app; general mobile-game monetization guidance recommends choosing a strategy (ads, IAP, subscription, or hybrid) early since it materially shapes UX decisions, and flags rewarded video as the least disruptive ad format for preserving player experience in a hybrid model ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/blog/tips-strategy/mobile-game-monetization/)). One caution worth building into the product's legal review from day one: several traditional morra variants are explicitly money-wagering games, and any mode resembling that inherits gambling regulation — the existing blockchain/dApp morra implementations all sidestep this by wagering tokens rather than real currency ([Algorand Developer Portal](https://developer.algorand.org/solutions/morra-game-using-reach/)).

## Synthesis: What This Means for Building the App

Several conclusions recur across all four research streams and should anchor the eventual product decisions.

**There is no single "correct" ruleset.** Finger range (0–5 vs. 1–5), call range, target score (10 through 21), and even whether "both correct" is a draw or a double-score vary by codified regional federation. The only responsible design is a configurable rules engine with named regional presets (FIGeST Italian, French *mourre*, Sardinian, Catalan, and a simplified "quick match" mode), rather than picking one tradition and presenting it as definitive.

**Team play must implement "holding the hand," not generic turn order.** This single mechanic is the most consistent, most distinctive rule found across every regional source and is what makes team morra strategically interesting; a naive round-robin implementation would misrepresent the game entirely.

**The hardest real-world problem — preventing mid-throw cheating — disappears in software**, but only if built correctly: server-authoritative resolution (never leak the opponent's move pre-reveal) is mandatory, and an optional cryptographic commit-reveal layer is valuable specifically for building trust in AI opponents and any competitive/wagered mode, with well-documented pitfalls (weak salts, selective-reveal timeouts) that must be designed around from the start rather than patched later.

**Existing precedent is thin but instructive.** A handful of small, mostly solo-developer apps (SaMurra, Morra vs AI, morra.io, handgames.app) already validate the concept and offer directly reusable UI and architecture patterns — particularly the two-row five-button input scheme and the bot-fallback-for-thin-liquidity approach — while leaving clear gaps (no pass-and-play mode, thin AI difficulty ladders, no localized dialect/voice packs) that a new entrant could differentiate on.

**The game's mathematical structure is a genuine asset, not just trivia.** Two- and three-finger morra have fully solved, provably optimal strategies that make excellent, honest tutorial content, and real behavioral research on expert versus novice randomness gives a concrete, evidence-based hook for both AI difficulty tuning and a differentiated "how random are you really" post-match feature.

**Framing and naming need care.** "Morra" collides in app-store search with "morra cinese" (rock-paper-scissors), the game's own governing body is FIGeST (not "Federmorra," which does not exist), and morra itself is not UNESCO-listed (only the broader Tocatì programme is) — all worth getting right in store listings, onboarding copy, and any "about the game" content to avoid repeating errors that are already widespread in secondary sources.
